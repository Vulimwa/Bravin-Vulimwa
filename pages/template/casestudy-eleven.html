The Real Problem

Post-conflict recovery involves organizations with different responsibilities, datasets, and operational timelines. Demining teams may manage hazard evidence, planners may manage infrastructure proposals, and investors may screen projects independently.

When those activities stay disconnected, project alignments can be planned without enough spatial risk context, field verification becomes difficult to track, and investment decisions lose the operational evidence behind them.

The Approach

I designed the Triple Nexus as a shared geospatial information environment. The system links three decision environments: a Demining Portal for hazard investigation and field verification, a Reconstruction Portal for project planning and delivery, and an Investor Portal for opportunity discovery and due diligence.

Rather than treating risk, infrastructure, and investment as separate datasets, the workflow carries spatial evidence through the lifecycle of a project. A proposed alignment can be tested, reviewed, assessed, and monitored while remaining available to the other portals.

What I Built

  1. Created connected portal experiences for demining, reconstruction, and investment stakeholders.
  2. Built a reconstruction workflow that captures proposed infrastructure as an actual polyline alignment.
  3. Persisted project geometry in ArcGIS Feature Services so it can be retrieved across applications instead of living only in a browser session.
  4. Connected hazard evidence, terrain, conflict history, infrastructure, field assessments, tasks, and work orders.
  5. Added spatial risk and priority outputs as decision-support indicators for investigation and planning.
  6. Designed dashboards, maps, reports, and monitoring views for operational and investment decisions.

What Changed

  • Recovery projects can be viewed with the spatial evidence needed to understand risk, context, and operational status.
  • Project geometry becomes authoritative, reusable information that can move between portals and teams.
  • The workflow creates traceability from proposal through review, field assessment, work orders, implementation, and investment consideration.
  • Analytical outputs support prioritization without being mistaken for direct confirmation of mine contamination or explosive hazards.
  • Shared services reduce information fragmentation and create a more continuous recovery workflow.
